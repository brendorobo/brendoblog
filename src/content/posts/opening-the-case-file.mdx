Every investigation starts the same way: you open a case, write down what you know, and
start documenting *everything*. This blog is that, applied to my own learning.

## Why write in public?

I work in security, and the part of the job that pulls at me most is **digital forensics and
incident response**: reconstructing what actually happened on a box from the traces left behind.
I'm now working toward the **GCFA** (SANS FOR508), and the fastest way I know to really learn
something is to explain it.

So the things I study, the CTFs I play and the investigations I practice will end up here as write-ups.

<Callout type="tip" title="the rule">
If I can't explain an artifact in plain language, with where it lives, what it proves and what it
*doesn't*, I don't understand it yet.
</Callout>

## What to expect

- **Study notes** for each FOR508 area: evidence of execution, lateral movement, memory, timelines, anti-forensics.
- **CTF write-ups**: how I worked through forensics challenges like HTB Holmes, including what I missed.
- **Interactive bits**: timelines you can filter, flashcards, and small tools when they help explain something.

## Status

Case is open. Evidence is being collected. Next up: evidence of execution.
