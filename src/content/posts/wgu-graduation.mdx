I just finished my Bachelor's in Cybersecurity and Information Assurance, and the path there was a little
backwards compared to most.

<Figure src="/images/linkedin/wgu-diploma.jpg" alt="WGU diploma, Bachelor of Science in Cybersecurity and Information Assurance" caption="Western Governors University, conferred September 29, 2026" />

## A backwards path

It started in my senior year of high school with an IT apprenticeship, where my title was Information
Technology Specialist. Not cybersecurity exactly, but it put me around real systems and real problems, and that
was enough to hook me. From there I studied up for my A+ and passed it while I was at Stark State. That cert
plus the hands-on experience was enough to land me a job as a cybersecurity analyst, and that's where the real
learning began.

## Where the real learning happened

These last two-plus years on the job have taught me more than any class could. Live incidents, real
investigations, actual tooling: you learn fast when it counts. So when I finally wrapped up the Bachelor's, I'll
be honest: most of what I know came from the work, not the coursework. The degree filled in some gaps, but
mostly it checks the HR box that opens doors, and there's real value in that.

<Callout type="tip" title="if you're starting out">
Get your hands on real systems as early as you can. An apprenticeship or help-desk role plus a cert like A+
opened more doors for me than any single class did.
</Callout>

## What's next

I'm a continuous learner at heart, so I'm not stopping here. I'm looking forward to starting my Master's in the
spring of 2027 and seeing where the next chapter takes me. In the meantime, I'm working toward the
[GCFA](/journey/).
